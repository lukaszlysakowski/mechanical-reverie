Week of 2026-10-19 → 2026-10-25

Three new mark-families, and a second substrate

The engine stopped being a single vocabulary on a single screen.

Engine v3.14.1 → v3.20.0 · Day 143 A Creative Partner · Day 144 Dragon Fold (dragon) · Day 145 Artificial Intelligence (scanline) · F-08 Departure Board · Day 146 The Reward (subdivide) · Day 147 Overstruck (lineprinter) — and the entire Plotter Studies track off Day 147.

The week in one line: the engine opened three new mark-families (fractal, raster, text) and then crossed into a second substrate (the pen), where its marks had to be re-learned, not just re-rendered.

Conceptual learnings

  1. Three veins opened in six days — the archive's biggest blind spots, closed. The engine had zero fractal/recursive forms, zero raster/video register, and zero text. Dragon (recursive path) + subdivide (recursive partition) opened fractals; scanline opened raster; lineprinter opened text. The lesson: when a pioneer's essence is a whole class the engine can't express, that's the signal to change the program, and the payoff is a vein, not a piece. Next absent classes to watch — sound/waveform, network/graph, moving-image loop.
  2. "Change the program, not the drawing" scales to a new SUBSTRATE. The deepest move wasn't a character — it was re-voicing the same genome in ink. The plotter work is all export-only: the raster never changed (7ddf2b3d held through fifteen engine versions). The program isn't "how the engine draws pixels" — it's "how a canonical seed becomes a mark," and a pen asks different questions of the same seed.
  3. The FORK RULE — the cleanest criteria-line we've drawn. A translation is a faithful export while it reproduces the parent's checksum (page, pitch, pen, caption are output-only). It becomes a study the moment a decision changes what is drawn. This gave the Plotter Studies track its spine and kept the daily archive's provenance clean.
  4. A medium teaches by failing. The first physical plot failed instructively: the digital ink-density ramp did not survive as a tonal order on paper — the pen read it as texture, not tone. That finding generated Study 02 (true solid black) directly. Principle: plot early, let the substrate contradict the screen, and treat the contradiction as the brief for the next study.
  5. Restraint lived in the RENDER, not in refusing the capability. Four new characters in five renders could have been creep. It wasn't — each debut was polarized to one signature (the new mark alone, other weights zeroed). The capability was justified by an absent class; the restraint was in the composition.
  6. The plotter is a DISCOVERY INSTRUMENT that feeds the main engine. Exploring Overstruck in ink surfaced a property the screen never exposed: the lineprinter's tone field has a latent two-tone structure. That discovery came home as a native screen capability (lpColorMode, below). The pen asks a question the pixel didn't → the answer becomes a dial on the main instrument.

Visual learnings

  1. Each new mark has a distinct, nameable signature — and reads cleanest ALONE.
    • dragon — deterministic order that looks grown (a space-filling fold).
    • subdivide — a quadtree whose cells halve and darken; directional bias makes it a trajectory (coarse→fine = a visual argument).
    • scanline — saturated horizontal bands interleaved with scan-text; the raster/CRT register.
    • lineprinter — the charset AS a tonal palette; hard-edge nested-rectangle tone from overstruck glyphs.
    The house pattern for any debut: polarize so the signature reads.
  2. On paper, the glyph ramp is a TEXTURE vocabulary, not a tonal one. Darkness order is not preserved by the pen — "tone" on screen becomes "pattern" in ink. To restore tonal depth you must change the fill (full-cell merge → true black). The single most important visual fact the plotter taught.
  3. PEN WEIGHT is a second compositional axis — the week's best new lever. Pairing a bold dark pen with a fine light one makes the two colours differ in weight as well as hue: the thin layer recedes, the bold layer advances. A two-colour plot gains real depth — not two flat layers, but a near-ground and a far-ground.
  4. Direction + colour = a WEAVE grammar. Splitting marks by orientation turns the # field into woven cloth; recolouring per cell gives textile structures — plain, swap (vertical emphasis), houndstooth (checker), twill (diagonal). The line-printer's crosshatch was always a latent weave; the pen made it literal.
  5. A solid on paper wants to MERGE, not tile. Per-cell inset hatch reads as stripes; full-cell fill at the pen pitch lets adjacent dark cells merge into true black. Small cores stripe more than large swatches. Rule: remove the inset for the darkest tier so neighbours join.

The build — two-colour ribbon (lpColorMode, v3.20.0)

The plotter's two-tone discovery wanted to be native to the screen, because red/black is the classic typewriter / teletype ribbon — a real piece of computer-art material history, not just a plot trick. So lineprinter gained lpColorMode ('figure' = dense glyphs in a 2nd ink; 'nested' = alternate by nested ring) + lpInk2. A future daily text source that is two-colour — a red/black ledger, a two-ribbon typewriter poem, teletype with red overprint — now renders as a canonical piece with a real checksum, not only as a plot. Default 'mono' is byte-identical (Day 147 still 7ddf2b3d). The first capability the plotter studies gave back to the daily engine.

What this generates next

  • Remaining absent classes (treat as priority, like fractals were): sound/waveform, network/graph, moving-image loop.
  • A Fractals room is overdue (dragon + subdivide live) — and a Text / Line-Printer room becomes defensible on the 2nd text day.
  • Plotter Studies open fronts: jet-black solid, a pen-first glyph vocabulary, >2 colours, and the richest — weight-contrast as a design primitive (every two-colour piece now asks "which layer recedes?").
  • Next text sources to watch: Knowlton EXPLOR, Houédard typestracts, Alison Knowles House of Dust (1967, first computer poem).

The through-line: a mark is not fixed — it changes meaning with the class it belongs to (fractal/raster/text), the substrate it lands on (pixel vs pen), and the weight that draws it. Three new dials on the same instrument.
